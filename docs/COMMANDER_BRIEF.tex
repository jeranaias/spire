  {\fontsize{11pt}{13pt}\selectfont\itshape A Contested-Logistics Decision Engine for the Stand-in Force}

\vspace{-2pt}
\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.4pt}

\vspace{2pt}
\noindent\textbf{BLUF.} A Marine S-4 forward in the First Island Chain
today cannot see Class~I, V, and~VIII supply on one screen --- those
classes live in three separate systems while GCSS-MC handles Class~IX
parts. SPIRE collapses every Marine logistics feed into one role-shaped
view, runs offline on a 2U box in a CONEX, records every decision in an
audit trail that cannot be quietly edited, and shares with coalition
partners in one click. \textbf{TRL~4, 1st place at Modern Day Marine 2026
(30~APR 2026). Operational pilot is the next step.}

\vspace{-2pt}
\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.4pt}

\section*{The Operator's Problem}
Today, a forward unit taking a Class~VIII demand spike under degraded SATCOM
has three systems open and a phone call. The planner has minutes --- not
hours --- to choose between cross-level, expedite, or emergency
requisition. The decision is unauditable; the artifact is a spreadsheet.

\section*{What SPIRE does}
Four role-shaped surfaces over one common picture of the unit:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Decision Bridge.} The home view. Five live signals on
    one screen: mission clock and FPCON, top alerts, forecasted Class~I
    /~V~/~VIII~/~IX shortages, mission-capable rate by unit with
    seven-day trends, and the health of the audit log itself.
  \item \textbf{PULSE.} Forecasts where shortages will hit and when,
    then drafts ranked options for the planner --- cannibalize,
    expedite, or cross-level --- sorted by which gets readiness back
    fastest. Predicts equipment failures days ahead and drafts the
    requisition before the asset breaks.
  \item \textbf{BASTION.} Common operating picture on a real geographic
    basemap with standard military symbols. Fuses access-control,
    drone, base-utility, and weather sensors into one threat picture.
    The operator drives the map by typing what they want to see.
  \item \textbf{SENTRY.} Automated classification of every record, with
    operator override at every step. Sanitized exports and one-click
    coalition release for FVEY, Japan, Australia, and the Philippines
    --- the receiving partner verifies they got exactly what was sent.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Under the hood}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Accepts data from any common source} --- file drops,
    secure transfer, email, polled web services, polled databases,
    streaming feeds. The four core Marine logistics systems (GCSS-MC,
    DRRS-MC) are pre-mapped. An on-board AI model handles unfamiliar
    files; mappings save per unit so the work isn't redone.
  \item \textbf{Audit by design.} Every change is cryptographically
    chained so an inspector can prove offline nothing has been altered.
    Feeds existing security monitoring tools.
  \item \textbf{Runs offline.} Fits on a 2U box in a CONEX. Decisions
    made forward during a SATCOM blackout reconcile automatically when
    comms restore. No cloud, ever.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Maturity \& Risk}
\textbf{TRL~4.} Validated at MDM~2026 against a synthetic GCSS-MC
dataset built to mirror the live export shape. The next step ---
TRL~5 --- requires a sanitized real GCSS-MC export from a sponsoring
MEF~G-4 or MLR~S-4. No ATO yet; the system is built to IL5 standards
(tamper-evident audit, role-based access, smartcard authentication,
no cloud) but is not formally accredited.

\section*{Why Now}
The 39th Commandant has named logistics the \textbf{pacing function}. The
\textit{Marine Corps Concept for Logistics} (Dec~2024) calls for resilience
through redundancy, dispersion, and innovation. The data-fragmentation gap
is named in published doctrine. The window to put SPIRE in operator hands
is the FY27 POM cycle.

\section*{Path Forward --- Ask}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{90-day operational pilot} with one MEF~G-4 or MLR~S-4
    willing to point a sanitized GCSS-MC export at the ingest path.
  \item \textbf{An MCWL evaluator} to red-team SENTRY against
    operator-malpractice patterns.
  \item \textbf{A POC at MCSC PM-MC2I or DC~I\&L} to translate operator
    requirements into a contract vehicle (SBIR Phase~II or OTA).
\end{enumerate}
